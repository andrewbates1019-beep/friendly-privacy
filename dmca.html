Friendly is owned and run personally by Andrew Bates, doing business as Friendly ("we", "us"). We respect copyright and expect everyone who uses Friendly to do the same. This page explains how to ask us to remove content you believe infringes your copyright, how to respond if your content was removed, and what happens to accounts that repeatedly infringe. It follows the US Digital Millennium Copyright Act ("DMCA"), 17 U.S.C. § 512.

1. Sending a takedown notice

If you believe content on Friendly (for example, a profile photo, bio or message) infringes a copyright you own or are authorized to act for, send a written notice to the contact in Section 5. The notice must include:

  1. Your physical or electronic signature (typing your full legal name works).
  2. A description of the copyrighted work you believe was infringed (or, if several works are covered by one notice, a representative list).
  3. A description of the content you believe is infringing and enough information for us to find it, such as the Friendly username of the account, and a description or screenshot of the photo or message.
  4. Your name, mailing address, phone number and email address.
  5. A statement that you have a good-faith belief that the use is not authorized by the copyright owner, its agent, or the law.
  6. A statement that the information in your notice is accurate and, under penalty of perjury, that you are the copyright owner or authorized to act on the owner's behalf.

A notice missing these elements may not be valid, and we may ask you to complete it. When we receive a valid notice, we will remove or disable access to the content and let the person who posted it know.

2. Sending a counter-notice

If your content was removed and you believe it was a mistake or misidentification, or that you had the right to use it, you can send a counter-notice to the contact in Section 5. It must include:

  1. Your physical or electronic signature.
  2. A description of the content that was removed and where it appeared before it was removed.
  3. A statement, under penalty of perjury, that you have a good-faith belief the content was removed or disabled as a result of mistake or misidentification.
  4. Your name, mailing address and phone number, and a statement that you consent to the jurisdiction of the federal district court for the judicial district where your address is located (or, if your address is outside the United States, any judicial district in which we may be found), and that you will accept service of process from the person who sent the original notice or their agent.

When we receive a valid counter-notice, we will send a copy to the person who sent the original notice. Unless they tell us within 10 business days that they have filed a court action to stop the infringement, we may restore the content 10 to 14 business days after we received your counter-notice.

3. False claims

Under the DMCA, anyone who knowingly misrepresents that content is infringing, or that it was removed by mistake, may be liable for damages. If you are not sure whether content infringes your rights, consider talking to a lawyer before sending a notice.

4. Repeat infringers

We close the accounts of people who repeatedly infringe copyrights. An account that is the subject of more than one valid takedown notice that is not successfully countered may be permanently closed. We may also close an account after a single notice in serious cases, and we may limit or remove content while we review a notice.
